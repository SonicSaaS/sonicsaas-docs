# Deployment & Adoption

**Weight: 5 of 100 — the smallest area.**

Every other area on this report asks whether Copilot would be *safe* to turn on. This one asks whether
it is actually being *used*.

It carries the least weight deliberately. Low adoption is a commercial problem, not a security one —
nobody is at risk because a licence is sitting idle. But it is worth measuring, because unused Copilot
licences are one of the easiest ways to waste a significant amount of money without noticing.

> **This area needs a live Copilot licence to measure.** Usage data only exists once Copilot is in use,
> so these two checks count only toward your **Full score** and are excluded from the
> **Readiness** score entirely. An organisation evaluating Copilot is not penalised for having no usage
> data yet.

## Copilot seats activated [#adoption-enabled]

**What this checks** — whether the Copilot licences purchased have been assigned to people.

**Why it matters** — an assigned seat is the difference between a licence being available and a licence
being paid for. Seats bought and never handed out are pure waste, and it happens more often than you
would expect — particularly where a block of licences was purchased ahead of a rollout that then
slipped.

**What "ready" looks like** — at least one purchased seat assigned to a user.

**How to fix it** — assign licences in the Microsoft 365 admin centre under **Users → Active users**.
If seats were bought for a rollout that has not happened, this is the prompt to either start it or
reduce the subscription at renewal.

**Microsoft's guidance** — [Set up Microsoft 365 Copilot](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-setup)

## Active Copilot usage [#adoption-active]

**What this checks** — how many people with a Copilot licence have actually used it in the last 30 days,
as a share of the seats assigned.

**Why it matters** — assigning a licence is not adoption. Copilot is a genuine change in how people
work, and without some encouragement a good proportion of users try it once and drift back to old
habits. This figure is the clearest early signal of whether a rollout is landing.

**What "ready" looks like** — half or more of assigned seats active in the last 30 days is a pass. Some
usage but below half is a warning. Assigned seats with no usage at all is a fail — that is money leaving
the business every month for nothing.

**How to fix it** — low usage is almost always a change-management problem rather than a technical one.
The things that reliably move this number are short role-specific training, a few internal champions
who use it visibly, and concrete examples of tasks it helps with. Reassign seats away from people who
have not touched it toward those asking for it.

**Microsoft's guidance** — [Microsoft 365 Copilot usage report](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/activity-reports/microsoft-365-copilot-usage)

## If these show "not measurable"

Usage reporting needs permission to read Microsoft 365 activity reports, and the administrator in the
delegated relationship also needs a reporting role such as Global Reader. Without it, usage cannot be
read and both checks are excluded from the grade rather than assumed to be zero.
